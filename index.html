Our story

A Barford landmark.

Behind the familiar sign is a building that has watched village life unfold for two centuries — first as a Georgian home and later as a pub woven into the everyday life of Barford.

Early 1800s

From Georgian house to village landmark

Historic England dates the main building to the early 19th century. Its formal central door, three-storey symmetrical frontage, sash windows and paired chimney stacks suggest a substantial private residence rather than a purpose-built inn. The first owner and the house's original name remain unknown.

1886

The traditional beginning of the pub

“Established 1886” is the date long associated with the Granville becoming licensed premises. It fits the surviving fabric: an early-19th-century house, followed by a late-Victorian red-brick extension recorded at its southern end.

From 1900

How the Granville got its name

Local history connects the name with the Reverend Edward Granville, who became Vicar of neighbouring Wasperton in 1900. The parish boundary then ran through or close to this part of Wellesbourne Road, suggesting the pub may have opened under another name before becoming The Granville Arms.

A working village

Serving Barford life

Wellesbourne Road was part of Barford's historic southern approach. Nearby homes doubled as workplaces for village trades, including tailoring and joinery. The pub grew within that living, working community, serving residents, labourers, tradespeople and travellers between Warwick and Wellesbourne.

The brewery years

Part of Warwickshire's brewing story

The Granville later appeared among the public houses associated with Flower & Sons, the great Stratford-upon-Avon brewery. It became part of the traditional network of brewery-owned village pubs that once connected communities across Warwickshire.

Village memories

Lemonade, Coca-Cola and a first pint

One resident remembered teenagers gathering opposite the pub and buying soft drinks through a small outside hatch—then returning to that same hatch on their 18th birthday for a first legal pint. It is a small story that says a great deal: the Granville belonged to village life even before you were old enough to enter the bar.

23 Jan 1987

Recognised for its heritage

The Granville Arms was awarded Grade II listed status for its special architectural and historic interest. The protected Georgian core survives alongside its Victorian side addition and later rooms created for dining, events and service.

Then & now

A room for every chapter of life

From everyday drinks and family meals to celebrations, wakes, music and quizzes, the pub has always been made for company. The rear function space—known first as the Back Room and later the Barford Barn—hosted parties, performances and village gatherings.

2024

A community that cares

When a change of tenant prompted concern, more than 1,000 people reportedly signed a petition and the local MP became involved. Whatever the business circumstances, the response showed how strongly Barford sees the Granville: not simply as a venue, but as a community asset.

Today

The story continues

The name and rooms have evolved through the years, but the purpose has endured. The Granville remains somewhere locals meet, families relax and visitors receive the kind of welcome that turns a pub into a local.
